# Market-Standard Terms Baseline (UK Venture, 2025–2026 market)

Use this as a comparison baseline when reviewing a term sheet. A term sheet is not "wrong" for deviating — but a deviation should be flagged and explained in plain English so the reviewing lawyer notices it deliberately rather than missing it.

## Priced equity rounds (SEIS/EIS, Seed, Series A+)
- **Liquidation preference:** 1x, non-participating is standard. Participating preference, multiples above 1x, or stacking preferences across rounds are non-standard and should be flagged.
- **Anti-dilution:** Broad-based weighted average is standard. Full ratchet is investor-favourable and non-standard for this stage.
- **Board composition:** Founder/investor board seats proportionate to round size; a single-investor board majority at Seed/Series A is unusual.
- **Protective provisions:** Standard list is: amendments adverse to the class, sale of company, senior/pari passu share issuance, debt above a stated threshold. Provisions reaching into ordinary-course operating decisions (e.g. hiring, spending below a low threshold) are non-standard and investor-favourable.
- **Information rights:** Threshold around 1–5% fully diluted is standard for quarterly management accounts; a materially lower threshold expands investor rights beyond norm.
- **Exclusivity:** 30–60 days is standard; anything longer without a clear reason should be flagged.
- **Founder vesting schedule:** 4 years total with a 1-year cliff is standard. Flag: shorter cliff (<12 months), longer total (>4 years), no vesting at all, or immediate full vest. Acceleration terms: double-trigger (vesting accelerates on sale + termination) is standard; single-trigger (founders fully vest on sale alone) is investor-unfavourable and misaligns post-acquisition incentives — flag as 🔴 Review.
- **Legal fees & expenses:** Company pays investor's reasonable legal fees, capped. Standard caps: £10k–£25k for early-stage/seed, £25k–£50k for Series A. Flag: no cap stated (🟡 Note), uncapped, or unusually high cap (>£50k for seed/A) as 🔴 Review.

## SAFEs
- **No interest, no maturity date** is standard (that's what distinguishes a SAFE from a convertible note) — a SAFE that includes either should be flagged as unusual/hybrid.
- **MFN (most favoured nation) clauses** are seen but not universal — always flag their presence explicitly, since they create ongoing obligations to retroactively reprice if a later, cheaper SAFE is issued.
- **Discount + cap both present** is common; a cap with no discount (or vice versa) is not unusual on its own.
- **Pro-rata rights** granted at SAFE stage are common in the current market but still worth surfacing, since they bind the cap table before there is one.

## Convertible loan notes
- **Interest rate:** Typically 6–10% per annum, accruing (not cash-paid) is standard.
- **Maturity date:** Should be present (12–24 months typical) — a convertible note with no maturity date blurs into SAFE territory and should be flagged as unusual.
- **Change-of-control premium:** A repayment premium on early exit (e.g. 1x–1.25x) is common; anything materially above that (1.5x+) is investor-favourable and should be flagged.
- **Security:** Unsecured is standard for early-stage notes; flag if secured (unusual at this stage, and relevant to the checklist's "loan agreements and other debt instruments" item).

## Universal flags (any instrument)
- Any clause referencing a "side letter" without the side letter itself being in the reviewed document set → flag as an omission risk (checklist item "Any side letters affecting share rights").
- Any term sheet condition precedent that references a document not found in the reviewed set (e.g. "clean disclosure letter", "confirmatory IP diligence") should be cross-checked against what's actually been supplied.
- Governing law other than England & Wales on an England-incorporated company is unusual and should be flagged.
