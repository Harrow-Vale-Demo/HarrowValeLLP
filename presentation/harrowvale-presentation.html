The skill lifecycle · create → prove → share

How a new skill is made and vetted

1 · Author writes three things

The instructions (SKILL.md), a strict output contract (the schema), and a handful of golden examples — good ones, and deliberately bad ones.

2 · Run the eval

One command runs the skill over those examples and scores it — precision and recall — across every term-sheet format.

3 · The gate decides

Meets the bar with no regression → cleared. Falls short → blocked, with the exact misses shown so they can be fixed.

4 · Publish

On a pass it's versioned and added to the shelf automatically — the same command does the eval, the version, and the publish.

No skill reaches a lawyer's desk until it passes — that is exactly what "approved" means here.
Security · taking the AI risk out

Why this isn't a black box

The usual worries — it makes things up, it's inconsistent, you can't see why — are designed out:

Fixed output shape

Typed fields, not free text. Removes inconsistent, unpredictable output.

No guessed numbers

If a figure isn't in the document, it's left blank — never invented. Removes hallucinated values.

Your standard, not its opinion

Checks against your BVCA baseline and Priya's checklist. Removes an AI forming its own view.

Locked & versioned

The gate blocks drift; a live matter pins a known-good version. Removes behaviour changing under you.

You stay the decision-maker: high-risk flags can't be silently approved, and every finding links to the exact source clause — so nothing is taken on trust.
Security · the sign-off answer

Where your client data goes

  • Not used for training. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, inputs and outputs on your Claude Team plan aren't used to train models by default.
  • Documents stay put. Files remain in your Microsoft 365 / SharePoint; a term sheet reaches Claude only when a lawyer runs a review.
  • The repo holds code, not client data. Test examples are synthetic; a firm rule keeps client documents out of it entirely.
  • Privilege protected. Run privileged material under zero-retention terms — with a short risk-allocation note for Priya.

Exactly where they live

One private home, one catalogue

  • The source of truth is your own GitHub repository, private to the firm — only Harrow & Vale can see it or install from it.
  • Each skill is a self-contained package — its instructions, its output contract, its reference checklist and its test set, versioned together.
  • A catalogue file (marketplace.json) lists the approved skills and their current versions — the firm's single shelf.
  • Every published version keeps its eval report, so you can always show which version was used and why it was trusted.
